1. Abstract
The introduction of the Transformer architecture revolutionized natural language processing by replacing traditional recurrent layers with multi-head self-attention mechanisms. In this paper, we explore optimization bottlenecks associated with scaling sequence lengths in transformer models. We demonstrate that context-aware semantic chunking combined with cross-encoder reranking drastically reduces retrieval noise and improves grounding faithfulness in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems.

2. Introduction
Traditional sequence-to-sequence models rely heavily on recurrent neural networks (RNNs) or LSTMs, which process text sequentially. This strict dependency prevents parallelization across training examples and struggles with long-range dependencies. The Transformer architecture completely bypasses recurrence, relying entirely on attention mechanisms to compute representations of inputs and outputs globally.

3. Methodology & Architecture
Our proposed research assistant framework integrates a multi-tier verification pipeline:
- Context-Aware Chunking: Rather than splitting text by arbitrary token lengths, documents are split based on embedding distance shifts using percentile thresholds.
- High-Volume Retrieval: The system casts a wide net, retrieving a candidate pool of k = 80 chunks to ensure relevant context is never omitted due to strict vector proximity dropoffs.
- Reranking Layer: A local cross-encoder (BAAI/bge-reranker-large) scores the 80 candidates and compresses them down to the top 5 most semantically dense segments.

4. Grounding and Guardrails
To eliminate hallucinations, the generation node utilizes zero-shot strict prompting combined with LangGraph conditional state routing. If retrieved document chunks lack direct semantic overlap with user queries, the pipeline intercepts the execution flow and safely triggers a fallback response: "I cannot answer this based on the provided documents."

5. Conclusion
Experimental results verify that high-volume candidate retrieval coupled with neural reranking and strict prompt constraints guarantees high fidelity, prevents extraneous information leakage, and establishes a production-grade environment for academic research assistance.
